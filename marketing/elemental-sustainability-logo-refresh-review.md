# Elemental Sustainability — Logo Refresh Creative Direction Review

Internal draft only — do not publish or send to client.

## Current identity baseline

- Blue abstract triangular / wave-loop icon at left.
- Large blue `Elemental` wordmark, spaced blue `Sustainability` below.
- Black tagline: `sustaining our tomorrow today` beneath a thin rule.
- Client feedback: explore a darker colour and/or subtle shading.

## Refresh directions

### 1. Deep Ocean Evolution — recommended for LinkedIn

**Idea:** Keep the existing icon and wordmark structure, but move the blue into a darker, more authoritative navy/teal-blue. Add only very subtle tonal depth inside the icon, not a glossy gradient.

**Rationale:** Best balances continuity with a more premium, credible sustainability feel. A darker mark will hold up better in small LinkedIn profile/avatar contexts and should feel more established without looking like a full rebrand.

**Brand risks:** If pushed too dark, it may lose the approachable environmental tone. Avoid corporate-finance navy; keep a slight teal or natural blue undertone.

### 2. Elemental Depth / Shaded Loop

**Idea:** Retain the current blue, but introduce soft internal shading or a two-tone treatment to the abstract loop/triangle so it feels more dimensional and modern.

**Rationale:** Directly responds to the client’s “maybe shading” note while preserving recognition. Useful if they like the current colour but want the icon to feel less flat.

**Brand risks:** Gradients can reproduce poorly in embroidery, small favicons, invoices, and low-resolution social placements. Over-shading could date the identity or make the icon look like generic tech/SaaS branding.

### 3. Earth + Water Accent System

**Idea:** Keep the main logo blue, but introduce a controlled secondary accent — muted green, sage, or aquifer teal — either in the icon overlap or as supporting brand colour, not across the full wordmark.

**Rationale:** Adds clearer sustainability signalling while keeping the existing equity in the blue mark. Could make presentation decks and social templates feel more distinctive.

**Brand risks:** Blue/green sustainability palettes are common; the combination must be restrained to avoid looking generic or NGO-like. Not the safest first move for the core LinkedIn profile mark.

### 4. Simplified Social Icon Crop

**Idea:** Create a dedicated LinkedIn-safe icon version: simplified loop mark, darker solid colour, no tagline, no thin rule, and no small `Sustainability` text inside the avatar crop.

**Rationale:** The existing full lockup is likely too detailed for a round/square LinkedIn profile image. A simplified icon-first version improves recognition at small sizes while the full logo can remain for documents and website use.

**Brand risks:** If the icon is not already recognised by the audience, using it alone may reduce immediate name clarity. Pair with consistent company page naming and banner artwork.

### 5. Refined Wordmark Hierarchy

**Idea:** Keep the icon almost unchanged, but modernise typography and spacing: slightly stronger `Elemental`, cleaner tracking on `Sustainability`, and a more integrated tagline/rule treatment.

**Rationale:** A conservative polish that improves professionalism without debating colour too heavily. Good if the client wants a refresh rather than a redesign.

**Brand risks:** Typographic changes can be subtle to the point of feeling underwhelming. Also less effective for LinkedIn profile use, where the tagline and secondary line will disappear.

## Recommendation

For LinkedIn profile use, lead with **Direction 1: Deep Ocean Evolution**, paired with **Direction 4: Simplified Social Icon Crop**.

This gives Elemental Sustainability a darker, more confident presence while keeping brand continuity. Use the full lockup for formal collateral, but use the simplified darker icon mark for LinkedIn avatars and small digital placements. Avoid heavy gradients; if shading is tested, keep it subtle and validate at favicon/avatar size before approval.
